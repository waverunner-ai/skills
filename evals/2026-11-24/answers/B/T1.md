AB05 isn't one of the 25 SEPA Direct Debit reason codes in the EPC guidance (EPC173-14, v8.1). I won't guess its meaning, because a wrong guess could lead you to retry a debit you shouldn't, or to give up on one you could collect.

Here is what I can say:

- **It's probably not a standard SEPA code.** Some banks and payment providers send their own internal codes, or put a code from another scheme or system in the return. Your bank may also have used a different code format from the one in the SEPA rulebook.
- **Check for a typo.** Codes close to it that are on the list include AC04 (closed account), AM04 (insufficient funds), MD01 (no valid mandate) and MS03 (reason not specified). I'm not saying AB05 is one of them. I'm only saying the original may have been misread.

**What to do:**
1. Ask your bank or payment provider what AB05 means. Ask for the ISO 20022 reason code behind it and the text description.
2. Look at the return message or file itself. It often has a plain-text reason next to the code, which you can compare against the standard codes.
3. Send me the file or the full return line if you can. It could be a pain.002, camt.054, camt.053 or CSV export. I can then check the other fields and give you a decision and a retry date if the code turns out to be a standard one.

**Until you know the reason, don't debit this customer again.** The right answer depends on the cause. Some causes, such as insufficient funds, allow a retry after a new advance notice. Others, such as no valid mandate or a refund claim, mean you must not collect again until you've sorted it out with the customer.

If the code does turn out to be a standard one, the earliest retry date is the end of a new 14-day advance notice, or a different period if you agreed one with the customer. It would also depend on a default waiting period, which is a Waverunner default, not an EPC rule.
