# AM04: insufficient funds

**What it means:** AM04 means "Insufficient Funds". The customer's account didn't have enough money to cover the 39 euro debit.

**Whose fault:** It could be either of you.
- **The customer:** their account was short on the collection date.
- **You:** you may have sent the pre-notification late or not at all. The customer could then have been caught out by a debit they weren't expecting.

Check when you told them about this debit before you assume it was their fault.

**Can you collect again:** Yes, but not straight away.
- **Limit:** Waverunner's default is at most 2 new attempts. That is a default, not a SEPA rule, because the EPC sets no retry rule.
- **Wait:** each new attempt goes out at least 5 days after you send the new pre-notification. The wait is also a Waverunner default.
- **Notice:** send the customer a new advance notice with the new date. The Core scheme asks for at least 14 calendar days' notice, unless you agreed a different period with them. So the earliest retry is the day that notice period ends. If you tell me when the notice goes out, I can work out the date. If you agreed a different notice period with the customer, the date changes with it.
- **Your provider:** check its own retry schedule before you add an attempt of your own. Stripe can retry at most twice within 30 days of the original attempt, if Direct Debit retries are enabled. Mollie may retry subscriptions up to 5 times, depending on the failure reason. These notes apply only if you use that provider.
- **MS03:** a bank can send MS03 ("reason not specified") in place of AM04. If you later get an MS03 on this debit, don't retry blind. Ask the customer or your provider which reason applies.

**Customer message you can send:**
> Your payment of €39 on [date] did not go through. Tell us here when your account will have funds: [link]. We will confirm the new collection date with you before we collect again.

Fill in the original collection date and put a link to a page in your shop for `[link]`.

Your bank or provider may also have charged a fee for the failed debit. I can add that to the picture if you tell me the amount.
