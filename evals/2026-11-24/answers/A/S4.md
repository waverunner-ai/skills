**MS03 = "Reason not specified"** (SEPA reason code, ISO 20022 external code set). It's a catch-all the debtor's bank uses when it rejects or returns a direct debit without giving a specific cause.

**Whose fault is it?**
Not determinable from the code alone. MS03 is deliberately vague. It's typically used when the debtor bank doesn't want to, or can't, supply a more precise code. Common real-world causes behind it:
- Account closed or blocked, but the bank used a generic code instead of AC04/AC06
- Insufficient funds (normally AM04, but some banks use MS03)
- Customer-side restrictions or a bank-internal rule
- Mandate problems the bank didn't classify (would normally be MD01 and the like)

So the fault can sit with the customer, their bank, or you (e.g. a mandate or format problem). Ask your own bank or PSP whether they have more detail, and contact the customer to find out what happened.

**Can you charge again?**
It depends on the underlying cause, and MS03 alone doesn't settle it:
- **Don't automatically re-present.** Under SEPA rules, you can only re-present a returned *collection* if the scheme permits it. Rejects and returns have different rules. A *return* (after settlement) generally can't be simply resubmitted as-is, while a *reject* (before settlement) may be fixable and resubmittable.
- **Check the mandate.** If the mandate is valid and the customer owes the money, you can usually ask them to pay by another route (bank transfer, card) or agree a new mandate/updated IBAN.
- **Avoid repeated attempts.** Repeated re-presentments on a generic failure code can lead to fees, scheme issues, or complaints. Only retry once you've confirmed the cause is resolved (for example, the customer has funded the account).
- **Refund-related returns are different.** If it was actually a customer-initiated refund (an unauthorized-collection claim), retrying would be inappropriate. Confirm with your bank first.

**Practical next steps:**
1. Ask your bank/PSP for the detailed reason or additional information attached to the return.
2. Contact the customer to confirm account status and that the mandate is still valid.
3. Based on the answer, either collect via another method, get a new mandate, or re-present once with the issue fixed.

Rules vary slightly by scheme (Core vs B2B) and by your bank's own terms, so confirm the specifics with them before resubmitting.
