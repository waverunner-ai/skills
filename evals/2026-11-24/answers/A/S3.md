**What MD06 means:** "Refund request by end customer." It's a SEPA Direct Debit return reason. The payer asked their bank to reverse a collection that had already been settled. It isn't a failed payment like insufficient funds (AM04) or a closed account (AC04). The 59 EUR was debited and then pulled back.

**Who caused it:** The payer, not their bank and not a technical fault. SEPA Core debits give the payer an unconditional refund right for 8 weeks after the debit, with no reason required and no check by the bank. For up to 13 months, they can claim a refund if there was no valid mandate. The code alone doesn't tell you which window or reason applied. Your payment processor's dispute detail may show the date gap, and sometimes a reason.

Common real-world causes:
- They don't recognize the charge or forgot they subscribed.
- They tried to cancel, couldn't, and used the refund as a cancellation.
- Real dispute or fraud (a stolen IBAN).
- Occasionally an honest mistake.

**Should you charge again?** I'd say no, not automatically.
- Re-presenting a debit the customer just reversed will likely trigger another MD06, or an MD01 claim ("no valid mandate"). Repeated refunds and unauthorized claims count against your creditor standing with your bank or processor.
- A refund within 8 weeks is final for that collection. You can't contest it through the scheme, and the money is gone.

What I'd do instead:
1. Pause or suspend the subscription so you don't keep collecting.
2. Contact the customer to find out why. If it was confusion or a cancellation attempt, that settles it.
3. If they owe the money and agree, collect it by another route: a card payment link or an invoice and bank transfer. A fresh debit on the same mandate is the riskiest option.
4. If they claim fraud or no mandate, don't re-debit. Check that you hold a valid mandate record (signed or accepted, with date and IBAN), since that's what you'd need if the dispute escalates.
