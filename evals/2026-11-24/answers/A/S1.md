**AM04 = "Insufficient funds."** The debtor's account didn't have enough money to cover the 39 € when the debit was presented. It's a SEPA reason code returned by the customer's bank.

**Whose fault?** Generally not yours. It's a balance problem on the customer's side, not a mandate or formatting error on your end. Nothing about your submission was wrong, though you'll still bear the consequences (the money isn't there, and your bank may charge a return fee).

**Can you just re-collect?** Usually yes, but with some care:

- **You can re-present, but SEPA rules limit this.** Under the SEPA Core scheme, a returned collection can't be simply resubmitted as a new "first attempt" in a loop. Many banks and creditors treat a returned AM04 as one retry opportunity at most, and some creditor banks restrict or disallow automatic re-presentment. Check your bank or PSP's terms.
- **If you retry, use the same mandate and the same amount** (39 €), and reference the original collection. Don't add fees to the amount unless your contract and mandate explicitly allow it.
- **Pre-notification still applies.** If you re-collect on a new date, the customer should get notice (the usual 14 days unless you agreed a shorter period), so tell them the new date.
- **Don't hammer it.** Repeated failed attempts can get flagged by your bank and raise your return ratio, which can put your creditor ID or account at risk.
- **Better approach:** contact the customer, tell them the payment bounced, and either agree a new collection date (a few days later, ideally after payday) or ask for a manual transfer or another payment method.

**Practical steps**
1. Mark the invoice as unpaid and note the AM04 return.
2. Message the customer promptly and neutrally; insufficient funds is often temporary.
3. Retry once at a sensible date, or switch to a transfer or card.
4. If it fails again, stop the debit and pursue it as a normal receivable (dunning).

Two caveats: the exact retry rules depend on your bank or payment provider, so confirm with them, and if you're collecting under SEPA B2B rather than Core, the rules differ somewhat. If you tell me which provider you use, I can be more specific.
