Not automatically, and I wouldn't do it right away.

**What MD06 means:** the customer asked their bank for a refund of a SEPA Direct Debit. Under the Core scheme they can do that within 8 weeks of the debit, no questions asked. If they claim the debit was unauthorised, they have up to 13 months.

**Why charging again immediately is risky:**
- MD06 is not a mandate cancellation (that would be MD01 or a revoked mandate), so the mandate may still be technically valid. But a refund is not a failed payment, so the usual re-presentment route (after a return or reject) doesn't apply. You would be issuing a brand-new collection.
- The customer has just shown they dispute or don't want this charge. Another debit will probably be refunded again, and you'll get the same MD06 plus possible fees.
- Repeated refunds can lead your bank or PSP to flag your account, and if the customer says the debit was unauthorised, collecting again could be a compliance problem.

**What I'd do:**
1. Contact the customer to find out why they refunded: a mistaken charge, a billing dispute, a cancelled service, or an unrecognised debit.
2. Check that your mandate is valid and that the customer hasn't revoked it.
3. If the debt is legitimate, send an invoice or payment link, or get a fresh mandate or other payment method.
4. If they confirm the debit was unauthorised, don't re-collect on that mandate.

I'm going from the general SEPA rulebook rather than your contract, so confirm the exact re-collection rules with your bank or PSP. If you'd like, I can look at how the refund is handled in this codebase, such as retry logic or mandate status, so MD06 doesn't trigger an automatic re-charge.
