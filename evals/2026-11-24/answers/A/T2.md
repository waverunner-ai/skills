Maybe, but don't retry blind. MS03 means "reason not specified by the debtor's bank," so the code tells you nothing about why it failed. What matters is what kind of R-transaction it came back as.

**1. Check the type in the status report (pain.002 or your bank portal)**
- **Reject or return** (e.g. the bank couldn't process it, or it bounced after settlement): you can usually re-present the collection under the SEPA rules.
- **Refund** (the debtor asked their bank to reverse it, within 8 weeks for Core): don't retry. The customer has effectively disputed the charge, and re-presenting it risks a second refund, a mandate dispute, or the bank flagging you.
- **Refusal** (the debtor's bank refused it before settlement, often because of a mandate block or debtor instruction): don't retry until you've confirmed the mandate is still valid.

**2. Ask your bank or the customer**
Because MS03 is vague, your bank can often tell you the underlying cause. It's also worth contacting the customer, since "not specified" frequently hides insufficient funds, a blocked account, or a deliberate reversal.

**3. If it's re-presentable**
- Keep the same mandate and amount. Don't add fees to the collection.
- Respect the pre-notification period you agreed with the customer, and re-submit in time for the collection cycle.
- Don't loop. Repeated failures can get your creditor ID flagged.
