**Short answer:** Probably nothing wrong on the customer's side, and no, don't retry next week.

**What AC04 means:** The debtor's account number is closed (ISO reason `ClosedAccountNumber`). The customer most likely switched banks or closed the account without telling you. It isn't a dispute, a refund request, or a funds problem. Those come back as MD01, MS02/MS03, or AM04.

**Why a retry won't work:** A closed account stays closed, so the same IBAN will fail again. Repeat submissions can also add bank fees and may flag you with your PSP or bank for poor mandate hygiene.

**What to do:**
1. Contact the customer, confirm the account was closed, and get the new IBAN.
2. Update the mandate. Under SEPA rules the mandate usually stays valid when the debtor's IBAN changes, but you must flag the amendment in the next collection (amendment indicator plus the original debtor account info). Many PSPs and banks would rather you collect a fresh mandate, so check what yours requires.
3. Resubmit the collection against the new account with a normal lead time (the usual 1 business day for Core, if your bank supports it).
4. Stop any scheduled recurring collections on the old IBAN so they don't keep bouncing.

**Edge cases:**
- If the customer says the account is still open, the IBAN may have been captured wrong. That would normally be AC01, but some banks return AC04 loosely, so verify with them.
- If the customer has stopped paying on purpose, closing the account is a known way to do it. Handle that as a collections or contract issue.
