**MD01 means "No mandate" in the SEPA Direct Debit scheme.** The debtor's bank is saying there is no valid mandate for the collection. That can mean:

- The customer says they never authorised it (an "unauthorised transaction" claim, which can be made up to 13 months after debit for Core).
- The mandate was cancelled or revoked.
- The mandate data you sent (mandate ID, signature date, creditor ID) doesn't match what the bank has. This is common for B2B, where the debtor bank must hold the mandate details.
- The mandate expired through 36 months of inactivity.

**Can you debit again?** Generally no, not on the same basis. Don't just re-present it.

1. **Check your mandate records first.** Look for a signed, unrevoked mandate and confirm you submitted the correct mandate reference, signing date and creditor ID.
2. **If the cause was a data or technical error on your side** (wrong reference, wrong sequence type, missing amendment indicator) and you hold a valid mandate, you can correct it and submit a new collection. Use the right sequence type, and keep the amount and the original due-date context consistent.
3. **If the customer disputes it or revoked the mandate**, don't debit again. A repeat collection without a valid mandate risks another return, fees, and a dispute with your bank. Get a new signed mandate, or collect the amount another way (invoice, bank transfer, card).
4. **Contact the customer** if you're unsure which case applies. The return reason alone doesn't say whether it was a dispute or a data mismatch.

Your own bank's SDD guidelines and your PSP's contract take precedence. If you're unsure, ask them whether re-presentment is allowed for this specific return. If you tell me where you're debiting from (country, Core or B2B), I can be more specific.
