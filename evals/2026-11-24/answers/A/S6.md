**MD07 means "Debtor deceased."** The debtor's bank is telling you the account holder has died, so it returned or rejected the SEPA direct debit.

**What to do**

1. **Stop all further collections.** Don't re-present the debit. A retry will just be returned again. Suspend any other scheduled or in-flight debits for this subscriber right away, since those will bounce with MD07 too.
2. **Cancel the subscription and mandate.** The SEPA mandate ends with the debtor's death. Mark it inactive in your system so it can't be used again, and stop any dunning or reminder emails.
3. **Be careful with the contact.** Don't send automated "payment failed" messages to the subscriber's address. If you want to confirm, write a short, sympathetic note to the account or a known contact, or wait for the family to get in touch.
4. **Handle any money owed.** If there's an unpaid balance, you can file a claim with the estate. Any future payment would need a new arrangement, such as a bank transfer from the estate, not a new debit on the old mandate. If any debits were collected after the date of death, expect to refund them.
5. **Tidy up the records.** Keep the mandate and transaction history for your normal retention period (usually the mandate lifetime plus 14 months for SEPA, and longer for accounting). After that, delete or anonymise the personal data per your GDPR obligations.

Banks occasionally return MD07 in error, but treat it as authoritative. Don't contact the subscriber's bank to dispute it. If someone from the family tells you the person is alive, ask them to sort it out with their bank and set up a new mandate.
