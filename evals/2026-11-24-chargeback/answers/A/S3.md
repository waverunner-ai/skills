**Will you be listed?** Probably, if the stated reason is excessive chargebacks. Mastercard's MATCH list (Member Alert to Control High-risk merchants) is maintained by acquirers. An acquirer is required to add a merchant within 5 days of termination if the termination was for a listed reason, and excessive chargebacks is one of them (reason code 04). A closure for another reason, like a business decision or inactivity, shouldn't be listed.

Ask your provider in writing:
- Whether it is a termination or a closure, and the exact reason given.
- Whether they will file a MATCH listing, and under which reason code.

Listings last 5 years.

**Can another provider take you?** Yes, but it is harder:
- Acquirers must check MATCH before boarding you. A hit is not an automatic ban, but mainstream processors and payment facilitators (Stripe, Square, PayPal and similar) will usually decline.
- High-risk acquirers will often accept listed merchants, typically with higher rates, a rolling reserve, lower volume caps, and sometimes a personal guarantee.
- Applications ask about prior terminations. Don't conceal the listing or the termination. Misrepresenting it can get you terminated again, and it is a fraud risk.

**What you can do now:**
- **Contest an error.** Only the listing acquirer can remove or correct an entry, and Mastercard won't do it for you. If the listing is wrong (for instance, the chargeback counts didn't meet the thresholds), dispute it with them in writing.
- **Get your numbers.** Ask for the chargeback counts and ratios they relied on, and check them against Mastercard's thresholds.
- **Fix the cause.** Chargeback causes include fraud, unclear billing descriptors, poor refund handling and subscription disputes. A documented remediation plan helps a lot with a new underwriter.
- **Use the lead time.** Start applying to high-risk providers before the account closes, so you don't have a gap in processing.
- **Check Visa too.** Visa has its own programs, and a termination may be reported there as well.

This is general information, not legal advice. Specifics vary by network rules version and region. If the listing materially affects your business, a payments lawyer or a high-risk payments broker can help with disputes and placement.
