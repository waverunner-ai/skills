**What it likely is**

Braintree usually passes through Visa network fees. A "monitoring" charge most likely comes from Visa's **VAMP (Visa Acquirer Monitoring Program)**, which replaced the older VDMP and VFMP programs in 2025. VAMP looks at your ratio of fraud reports and disputes to settled card-not-present transactions in a month. If you cross the threshold, Visa fines the acquirer, and that cost is passed on to you as a fee.

**Approximate figures, which have changed over time**
- The "excessive" merchant threshold was about 2.2% in 2025 and is scheduled to drop to about 1.5% in 2026. There is also a minimum monthly count, around 1,500 fraud and dispute events.
- Per-event fees above the threshold have been in the range of roughly $5-$10 per fraud or dispute event. I'm not confident of the exact number or of what applies to your region and tier.
- Even below the threshold, an "early warning" or "needs improvement" status can sometimes trigger notices.

**How to check it**
1. In the Braintree Control Panel, open your **monthly invoice or billing statement** and find the line item. It should name the program and the month it covers.
2. Look at **Transactions and Disputes** for the same period, and count the chargebacks and fraud-flagged transactions against your settled volume.
3. Download the **settlement and disbursement reports** to see whether the fee was deducted from payouts.
4. Open a ticket with **Braintree Support**. Ask which Visa program triggered it, which month and ratio it was based on, and the per-event rate. Ask what you can do to get out of the program.

**How to avoid it**
- Respond to disputes quickly, and use Braintree's Kount or fraud tools.
- Use 3D Secure on risky transactions.
- Make your billing descriptor recognizable, since unrecognized charges often become disputes.
- Refund obvious problem orders before they turn into chargebacks.
