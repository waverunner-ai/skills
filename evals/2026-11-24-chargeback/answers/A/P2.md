These are the figures as I know them, so check them against the current Visa and Mastercard rules or your acquirer's documentation, since both programs have changed recently.

**Mastercard (Excessive Chargeback Program)**
- Ratio = chargebacks received this month ÷ sales transactions from the previous month.
- Thresholds are usually 100+ chargebacks and 1.5% for "Excessive Chargeback Merchant", and 300+ chargebacks and 3% for "High Excessive".
- It counts chargeback volume only. Fraud reports that never became disputes are not in the ratio.

**Visa (VAMP, which replaced the older VDMP and VFMP programs)**
- Ratio = (fraud reports (TC40) + disputes (TC15)) ÷ settled card-not-present transactions, in the same month.
- Because fraud reports count even when no chargeback follows, it is typically higher than a plain chargeback ratio.
- Visa has set the "Excessive" merchant threshold at about 2.2%, with a planned cut to about 1.5% in 2026 that varies by region. Confirm the current figure with your acquirer.

**Differences that matter**
1. **Numerator:** Mastercard counts chargebacks. Visa counts disputes plus fraud reports.
2. **Denominator:** Mastercard divides by the prior month's sales. Visa divides by the same month's settled CNP transactions.
3. **Dollar amount:** Neither uses dollar value. Both are count-based.
4. **Alerts:** Some pre-dispute alerts, such as Ethoca, CDRN and RDR, can keep a case from becoming a chargeback. How each network counts them differs, so ask your acquirer.
