Your ratio is 12 ÷ 4,000 = **0.30%**. That is comfortably under the usual thresholds, so you're probably fine, with a few caveats.

**Why it looks fine**
- Card network monitoring programs typically start around 0.9% to 1.5%, and they usually also require a minimum count (often 100 chargebacks a month). At 12, you're well below both.
- Many processors, such as Stripe, Adyen and Square, set their own limits, commonly around 0.75% to 1%. 0.30% is under those as well.

**Caveats**
- **Mismatched periods.** Mastercard divides this month's chargebacks by last month's transactions, which is what you did. Visa and some processors divide by the same month's transactions. Check which method your acquirer uses. With similar volume the difference is small.
- **Visa counts more than chargebacks.** Under VAMP, early fraud reports (TC40) count along with disputes, so your Visa ratio can be higher than 0.30%. Check your processor dashboard for the figure they actually report.
- **The month isn't over.** Disputes arrive weeks after the sale, so the real number for this month's sales will probably be higher. Watch the trend, not just one month.
- **Small volume is volatile.** At 4,000 transactions, about 24 more chargebacks would put you at 0.9%, and 4 or 5 more is only a 0.1% change. A single fraud ring or a billing-descriptor problem can move you fast.
- **Use your own thresholds.** Your processor's contract may be stricter than the network rules, and the exact numbers change, so confirm them with your acquirer.
