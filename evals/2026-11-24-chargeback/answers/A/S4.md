**What counts as a good month (Mastercard ECP)**
- **Excessive Chargeback Merchant (ECM):** 100-299 chargebacks in a month *and* a ratio of 1.5% or more.
- **High Excessive Chargeback Merchant (HECM):** 300 or more chargebacks *and* a ratio of 3.0% or more.
- The ratio is this month's chargebacks divided by *last* month's transaction count.
- A good month means falling below the ECM tier, either under 100 chargebacks or under 1.5%. The count of consecutive good months resets if you slip back over.

**What changes the timeline**
- **Lag:** Cardholders can dispute for up to about 120 days, so chargebacks from past bad sales keep arriving after you fix the cause. Expect the ratio to improve a month or two after the fix.
- **Time already served:** The clock starts from your first month under the threshold, not from the flag date.
- **Fees and escalation:** While you're in the program, your acquirer can pass on assessments, which can rise with each month in the program and by tier. Staying in for longer than about 6 months can also lead to a risk review or termination.
- **Acquirer terms:** Your processor may add stricter rules or a longer probation period than Mastercard's minimum.

**Next steps**
1. Check your notice or ask your acquirer for your current tier, your ratio for the last few months, and their exit criteria in writing.
2. Compare your monthly chargeback count against 100 and your ratio against 1.5%, since either one gets you out of the ECM tier.
3. Use chargeback alerts (Ethoca, Verifi) and refund-before-dispute tools to catch disputes early. They also help with Visa's programs.
