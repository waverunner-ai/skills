Probably not. A refund before a dispute is filed keeps the dispute out of your count, but it likely doesn't keep the fraud report out.

Under Visa's VAMP (which replaced VDMP and VFMP), the merchant ratio is roughly (fraud reports + disputes) ÷ settled card-not-present transactions. Those are two separate counters:

- **Disputes (TC15):** If you refund before the issuer files a chargeback, there is no dispute, so nothing is counted here.
- **Fraud reports (TC40):** When a cardholder tells the bank "this was fraud," the issuer typically reports it to Visa, often independent of whether a dispute follows. That report is generally counted against you even if you refund the money afterward. Refunding doesn't retract it.

So the refund saves you the chargeback and its fees, but the transaction can still count in your ratio through the fraud report.

What can keep it out of the ratio is stopping it earlier. Disputes resolved through Visa's RDR (Rapid Dispute Resolution) are excluded from the dispute side. Order Insight and similar pre-dispute tools can also deflect the dispute. These are set up through your acquirer or a provider like Verifi, and they work on disputes, not on TC40 fraud reports.

Thresholds, exclusions and counting rules have changed several times recently, so confirm the current details with your acquirer or processor. Ask them directly whether a TC40 on a transaction you already refunded still counts toward your VAMP ratio. Your VAMP dashboard should show which transactions are being counted.
