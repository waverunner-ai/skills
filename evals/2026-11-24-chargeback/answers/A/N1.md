**Ratio: 90 ÷ 6,000 = 1.5%**

**You're not in Mastercard's excessive tier, but you're close.** As I understand the Excessive Chargeback Program (ECP), a merchant needs both of these to be classed as an Excessive Chargeback Merchant (ECM):

- At least 100 chargebacks in the month
- A chargeback-to-transaction ratio of at least 1.5%

You meet the ratio, since 1.5% is right at the threshold, but you're 10 chargebacks short of the count. The High Excessive tier (HECM) needs 300 or more chargebacks and a ratio of at least 3%, so you're well clear of that.

- **You're about 10 chargebacks from ECM.** A slightly worse month, or slightly lower sales volume, would put you in it.
- **Mastercard divides this month's chargebacks by last month's transactions.** That matches how you framed it (90 against 6,000), but check which transaction count your acquirer uses.
- **Check the thresholds with your acquirer.** I'm going from my knowledge of the program rules, and acquirers can set stricter internal limits or monitoring triggers than Mastercard's own.
- **Look at Visa separately.** Visa's programs use different metrics and thresholds, so a safe Mastercard position doesn't mean you're safe there.

At 1.5% you should be actively reducing chargebacks, for example with clearer billing descriptors, faster refunds, and dispute-alert services, before volume shifts and pushes you over.
