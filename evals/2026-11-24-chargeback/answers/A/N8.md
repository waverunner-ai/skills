**What it likely costs**

Cost = 12 × the per-count fee on your Braintree statement. Visa's VAMP (Visa Acquirer Monitoring Program) fees are a few dollars to around ten dollars per fraud or dispute count. At $4 that's $48, and at $10 it's $120. Those rates are only to show the arithmetic, so don't treat them as your bill. Braintree may also pass the fee through with its own markup. Standard dispute fees on those same cases are separate and come on top.

**What to check**

1. **Which tier is yours.** "Above Standard" describes your acquirer's portfolio tier. It does not automatically mean your own merchant ratio is bad. Ask Braintree whether you are individually flagged or are being billed as a pass-through of the acquirer's penalty.
2. **Your own VAMP ratio.** It is roughly (fraud reports + disputes) ÷ settled Visa transactions for the month. Compute it yourself from the Braintree dashboard and compare it with the current thresholds, which Visa has been tightening. Confirm the numbers on Visa's and Braintree's current pages.
3. **What the 12 counts are.** Ask which transactions, which month, and whether they are fraud reports (TC40), disputes (TC15), or both. Check for duplicates, such as one transaction counted as both a fraud report and a dispute.
4. **Whether any were avoidable.** Disputes resolved through Visa's pre-dispute tools (RDR, CDRN, Order Insight) may be excluded or reduced. Check whether you're enrolled in those through Braintree.
5. **The fee basis in your contract.** Look for the pass-through clause in your Braintree/PayPal agreement. Check whether the fee applies per count from the first count or only above a threshold, and whether you can get a waiver.
6. **Escalation.** If this repeats, look for an enforcement timeline or remediation plan requirement. Ask Braintree what ratio gets you out of the flagged status.
7. **Root cause.** Review the 12 cases for patterns: card testing, friendly fraud, unclear billing descriptors, or a refund process that is too slow. Fix whichever pattern shows up before next month's count.
