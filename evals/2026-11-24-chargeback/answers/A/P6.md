Both are tiers of Mastercard's Excessive Chargeback Program (ECP). The difference is severity: HECM is the higher tier, with heavier fees and closer scrutiny.

| | **ECM** (Excessive Chargeback Merchant) | **HECM** (High Excessive Chargeback Merchant) |
|---|---|---|
| Chargebacks in a month | 100-299 | 300 or more |
| Chargeback-to-transaction ratio | 1.5% (150 bps) or higher | 3.0% (300 bps) or higher |

You need to meet both the count and the ratio to land in a tier. A merchant with a 5% ratio but only 40 chargebacks is not in either.

**How the ratio is calculated:** chargebacks received in the current month divided by the number of Mastercard transactions in the previous month.

**Consequences:**
- **ECM:** The acquirer has to put the merchant on a remediation plan. Mastercard assesses the acquirer, and the acquirer usually passes the cost through to you.
- **HECM:** Fees escalate with each month in the program. Repeated or prolonged HECM status can lead to the acquirer terminating the merchant, and you can end up on the MATCH list.

**Caveats:**
- Exact fees, how long you stay in the program, and the exit criteria vary by region and change over time. Check with your acquirer or the current Mastercard Security Rules and Procedures for your region.
- Mastercard runs a separate Excessive Fraud Merchant (EFM) program for fraud-related chargebacks, which uses different thresholds.
