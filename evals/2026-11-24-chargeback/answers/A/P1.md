No, 1% is a rule of thumb, not a universal cutoff. It's popular because it sits near the point where many processors start paying attention, but there's no single number that triggers a shutdown.

**Card network programs** (these trigger fines and monitoring, not necessarily termination):
- **Mastercard** Excessive Chargeback Program: roughly 1.5% and 100 chargebacks in a month. The higher tier is about 3% and 300.
- **Visa** replaced its older dispute programs with VAMP in 2025. The thresholds are in the low single digits, around 2.2% for merchants, and Visa has announced tightening them. The old VDMP figure was 0.9%.

The exact figures and effective dates have been shifting, so check the networks' current published rules.

**Your processor's own rules** matter more for whether your account is shut down. Stripe, PayPal, Square, Shopify Payments and others set their own thresholds, and they often act well below the network limits. Many start reviewing at 0.5-0.75%.

**Other factors that decide whether you get cut off:**
- **Count and volume:** 3 chargebacks on 100 orders is 3%, but processors rarely act on numbers that small. High-volume merchants get scrutinized at lower percentages.
- **Industry risk:** Subscriptions, digital goods, travel, supplements and other high-risk categories get less tolerance.
- **Trend:** A rate that's climbing fast is treated worse than a steady one.
- **Fraud-type disputes:** Separate fraud ratios can trigger action on their own.
- **Calculation method:** Some processors divide this month's chargebacks by this month's transactions, others by the prior month's, so the same shop can look different under different rules.
- **Your history:** New accounts and accounts with prior warnings get less slack.

In practice, aim to stay under about 0.5%, respond to every dispute, and use alerts or refunds for early resolution (Verifi, Ethoca). Also read your processor's terms, since their number is the one that counts for your account.
