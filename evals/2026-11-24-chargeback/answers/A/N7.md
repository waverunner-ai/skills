**Your Visa ratio is 2.2%** (220 bps): (60 disputes + 50 TC40 fraud reports) ÷ 5,000 settled CNP transactions = 110 ÷ 5,000.

**Are you in Visa's merchant programme? Almost certainly not.** Visa's current programme, VAMP (Visa Acquirer Monitoring Program), replaced the old VDMP and VFMP. As I understand it, a merchant is identified as "Excessive" only if both conditions hold:

1. The ratio is at or above the merchant threshold. This was 2.2% in 2025 and is lower in 2026 (1.5% in the regions where the step-down applies, which I believe includes the EU).
2. The merchant has at least 1,500 combined fraud reports and disputes in the month.

You exceed the ratio threshold, but with 110 combined you are far below the 1,500 count minimum, so you shouldn't be flagged. Exact thresholds and effective dates vary by region and have changed during the rollout, so check the current Visa rules or ask your acquirer or PSP to confirm.

**Why you should still act:**
- A 2.2% ratio is high. If your volume grew roughly 14x (to about 1,500 fraud reports and disputes a month) at the same rate, you would be in the programme.
- Your acquirer is monitored at a much stricter level (about 0.5-0.7%) and may impose its own limits, such as reserves, fees, or termination, well before Visa's merchant threshold applies.
- Check whether your PSP's dashboard counts the ratio the same way. Some use the dispute count only, or a different denominator.

Dispute-prevention tools such as Verifi RDR or CDRN, Order Insight, and 3-D Secure (SCA is already required in the EU) can cut the count quickly. They would also help your acquirer relationship.
