A dispute rate under 1% doesn't protect you from a payout pause. Stripe's risk and compliance reviews run separately from the card-network dispute thresholds, and the document request suggests this is one of those reviews.

**Why a pause can happen at 0.4%**
- **Underwriting or verification review.** Stripe may want to confirm your business, such as your website, products, terms, identity or bank details. This is common for new accounts, changed business details, or a sudden jump in volume or average ticket size.
- **Business model mismatch.** Activity that looks different from what you described at signup, or that falls into a restricted or higher-risk category, can trigger a hold.
- **Delivery risk.** If you charge upfront for things delivered later (pre-orders, subscriptions, events, services), Stripe may want proof you can fulfil them before releasing funds.
- **Other signals.** Refunds, early fraud warnings, a spike in fraud reports, or a pending dispute balance that is large relative to your volume can each prompt a review, even if the headline rate looks fine.
- **The dashboard number may not be the one that matters.** It's usually disputes divided by charges over a window and lags behind. Card networks, such as Visa's VAMP program, also count fraud reports that never became formal disputes, and they use a different denominator. Your network-side ratio can be noticeably higher than 0.4%.

**What to do**
1. Open the notice in your Dashboard or email. It normally states the reason and what documents are accepted, which tells you which of the above applies.
2. Upload exactly what it asks for, such as ID, business registration, bank statement, proof of delivery, or website and terms. Missing or mismatched documents are the most common reason a review drags on.
3. Watch the deadline. Missing it can lead to a longer hold or account restrictions.
4. If the reason isn't clear, contact Stripe support from the Dashboard and ask which specific review this is and which signal triggered it.
