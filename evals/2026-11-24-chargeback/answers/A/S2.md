Almost certainly not, at your volume, but check what your provider's 1.6% actually measures.

**Why it probably doesn't apply:** Visa's current programme is VAMP (Visa Acquirer Monitoring Program), which replaced the older dispute and fraud monitoring programmes. A merchant's VAMP ratio is roughly (fraud reports + disputes) ÷ settled card-not-present transactions in the month. A merchant is only classed as "excessive" if it exceeds both a ratio threshold and a minimum count. As I remember it, the ratio threshold has been around 2.2% and is due to tighten to about 1.5% in some regions during 2026, and the count minimum is about 1,500 fraud and dispute events a month.

At 800 sales a month, 1.6% is about 13 fraud reports or disputes. That is far below any count minimum I know of, so you shouldn't be in Visa's excessive tier on that number.

**What to check with your provider:**
- **What the 1.6% is.** It could be a fraud ratio, a dispute ratio, or the combined VAMP ratio. It could also be their own internal risk metric rather than Visa's.
- **Whether they apply their own limits.** Acquirers and payment providers often set stricter thresholds than Visa for small merchants, and they can add reserves, fees or warnings on their own. That is the more likely source of any pressure on you.
- **The current thresholds.** I'm working from memory, and Visa has revised these figures several times. Ask for the exact ratio and count thresholds that apply to you in the EU.

**At low volume:** a handful of disputes can swing your ratio a lot (3 more disputes would move it by about 0.4 points). It's worth responding to disputes promptly and tightening fraud screening, such as 3-D Secure, which also shifts fraud liability to the issuer in the EU.
