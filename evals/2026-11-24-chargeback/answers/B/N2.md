**Ratio: 1.53% (153.1 bps)**, which is 1,500 chargebacks over 98,000 transactions. It's counted the way Mastercard counts it: this month's chargebacks divided by last month's transactions. The count is by transaction number, not sales revenue.

- **ECM and HECM sources:** these figures come from TabaPay's summary, not Mastercard's own rule text. The counting method comes from Verifi.
- **Assumption:** the comparison assumes both numbers are Mastercard-only and for one merchant ID. If they are all-card totals or cover several merchant IDs, treat the result as an estimate.
- **MATCH timing:** it only comes into play when you or your acquirer decide to end the account. The acquirer then reports to MATCH within 5 calendar days of the decision.
- **Visa:** its merchant test applies only while your acquirer is below both Visa acquirer levels. The CEMEA (Central Europe, Middle East and Africa) region has different figures: 220 bps, 150 events and USD 75,000.
- **Won disputes:** a dispute you win still counts in the monitoring totals.

**What the sources list for ECM**
- Per TabaPay, ECM comes with a remediation plan, and assessments start from month 2.
- Per TabaPay, leaving ECM takes three consecutive months below the thresholds.
- That is the programme's published follow-up. It isn't a prediction for your account.

**What to ask your provider**
- Which data month, merchant ID and counts its figure used.
- Which test applies to you.
- Your VAMP ratio and your acquirer's Visa level.
- If you've had a warning or fee, the ratio and calculation behind it, what remediation and evidence it wants, and its deadline.

This is an estimate. Your provider confirms which test applies and whether Mastercard counted you. For anything beyond that, such as a MATCH listing or a closure, ask a lawyer.
