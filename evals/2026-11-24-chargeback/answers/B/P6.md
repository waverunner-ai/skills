Mastercard's two levels differ in how high the ratio and the chargeback count have to be. HECM is the more severe level. To be in either one, you have to meet both a ratio line and a minimum count in the same month.

| | **ECM** (Excessive Chargeback Merchant) | **HECM** (High Excessive Chargeback Merchant) |
|---|---|---|
| Ratio | 150 bps (1.5%) or more | 300 bps (3%) or more |
| Minimum count | At least 100 chargebacks in the month | At least 300 chargebacks in the month |
| Follow-up listed for the level | A remediation plan, with assessments from month 2 | Larger assessments than ECM, from month 3 |

**How the ratio is counted.** Mastercard divides this month's Mastercard chargebacks by last month's Mastercard transactions. A July ratio uses June's transactions. It counts transactions, not sales revenue, and it applies to one merchant ID.

**Both parts must be met.** A small account can pass the ratio and still miss the minimum. For example, 45 chargebacks on 2,500 transactions is 180 bps (1.8%). That is above the 150 bps line but below the 100-chargeback minimum.

**How you leave.** Per TabaPay, you need three consecutive months below the thresholds before Mastercard removes you from the programme. Your provider still decides for itself about restrictions and about reopening a closed account.

**Caveats**
- These figures come from TabaPay's *Network Monitoring Programs* page, a payment processor's summary, read 4 November 2026. They are not Mastercard's own rule text. The way Mastercard counts comes from Verifi's FAQ, read 30 October 2026.
- The guide doesn't state a region or effective date for ECM and HECM.
- A dispute you win still counts toward the totals.
- Your dashboard rate may use different months from Mastercard's count.
- This is a ratio and a published line, not a verdict. Only your provider can confirm whether Mastercard counted you.

**Related programmes.** MATCH reason code 04 is separate. It is a screening list for closed accounts, with a line of over 1.5% and at least USD 5,000 of chargebacks. Visa's VAMP merchant test is also separate. It counts fraud reports as well as disputes, so these numbers don't give you a Visa ratio.

**What to ask your provider**
- Which test applies to you, and on which merchant ID and data month it was measured.
- Which data month, merchant ID and counts its figure used.
- Which months its dashboard rate and the network's count used.
