**No. A dispute you win still counts in the monitoring totals.** That is the guide's rule (Waverunner, "What chargeback ratio gets my account shut down?", updated 6 November 2026). The guide states it without a named source.

- **Mastercard:** The ratio is this month's chargebacks divided by last month's Mastercard transactions. The guide's counting method says nothing about taking out disputes you later win. Its Mastercard figures come from TabaPay (a payment processor's summary, read 4 November 2026) and Verifi (read 30 October 2026), not from Mastercard's own rule text.
- **Visa (VAMP):** The ratio counts card-not-present fraud reports plus disputes. Fraud reports (TC40s) count even if no dispute follows, unless they qualify for Visa's Compelling Evidence 3.0 exclusion (per Shopify's page, read 4 November 2026).
- **Rapid Dispute Resolution (RDR):** Disputes settled through this pre-dispute service stay out of the VAMP ratio, depending on when the data is pulled (per Checkout.com, read 4 November 2026). That is different from winning a dispute after it has been filed. Ask your provider whether it offers RDR.
- **Your dashboard may differ:** Its rate may use different months from the network's count. Stripe's network totals can also include disputes that its dashboard rate leaves out.

Every published line is a ratio and a minimum count, so a high win rate doesn't change how the lines apply to you. Mastercard's ECM level, for example, needs at least 150 bps and at least 100 chargebacks in the month.

**What to ask your provider:**
- Which test applies to you, and on which merchant ID and data month it was measured.
- Your VAMP ratio and your acquirer's Visa level.
- Which months its dashboard rate and the network's count used.
