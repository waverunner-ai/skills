## How Mastercard counts it
Mastercard divides this month's chargebacks by last month's transactions. A July ratio uses June's transactions (per Verifi, read 30 October 2026). Your dashboard rate may use different months, so ask your provider which months it used.

## How Visa counts it
Visa's measure is called VAMP. It isn't chargebacks alone. It counts card-not-present fraud reports (TC40, also shown as early fraud warnings) plus disputes, divided by settled transactions. The guide doesn't say which month's settled transactions Visa divides by. I can't work out your VAMP ratio from two numbers, because only your provider has the fraud-report count.

Two other points apply to both networks:
- A dispute you win still counts in the totals.
- Every published line has a ratio and a minimum count. A small account can pass the ratio and still miss the minimum.

| Mastercard MATCH code 04 | Over 1.5% | At least USD 5,000 of chargebacks | Mastercard rules, read 30 Oct 2026 |

- **ECM and HECM:** These figures come from TabaPay, a payment processor's summary, not from Mastercard's own rule text.
- **Visa's test:** It applies only while your acquirer is below both Visa acquirer levels (50 bps and 70 bps across the acquirer's whole portfolio).
- **MATCH:** It compares the previous 3 months of chargebacks against one month's sales transactions. It matters only when you or your acquirer end the account. The guide doesn't say which month's sales it uses.
- **Visa regions:** The guide names regions only, so I can't tell you which Visa region your country falls in.

None of these is a universal shutdown line. Shopify, for example, can restrict an account before any network threshold is reached.

## What to ask your provider
- Which test applies to you, and on which merchant ID and data month it was measured.
- Your VAMP ratio and your acquirer's Visa level.
- Which months its dashboard rate and the network's count used.
