**MATCH** is Mastercard's merchant screening list. Acquirers (the banks or processors that settle card payments) check it before they onboard a merchant. I can't tell you whether you're on it, and neither can any tool I have. Only your provider can confirm that, or Mastercard through an acquirer.

**What the guide says about reason code 04 (excessive chargebacks)**
- **When it applies:** only when you or your acquirer decide to end the account. A freeze alone doesn't trigger it.
- **The test:** chargebacks over the previous 3 months exceed 1.5% of one month's Mastercard sales transactions, and the chargebacks total at least USD 5,000. Both parts must be met. The guide doesn't say which month's sales it uses, so your provider has to say.
- **Reporting:** if your acquirer has reason to believe you qualify, it must report you within 5 calendar days. The clock starts at the earliest of its termination decision, your notice, or when it learns you qualify.
- **Effect of a listing:** Mastercard allows another acquirer to accept a listed merchant, but each acquirer makes its own decision. Your old provider's closure decision stands.
- **Source:** Mastercard's Security Rules and Procedures, Merchant Edition, read 30 October 2026.

**Your situation**
- A frozen account is not the same as a closed one. The guide says a provider such as Shopify can restrict an account before any card-network threshold is reached.
- If you were only frozen, the MATCH trigger (an account being ended) may not have happened. If your provider has closed the account or told you it will, a listing is possible.

**What to ask your provider**
1. Are you ending the account, or only restricting it?
2. Have you reported me to MATCH? If so, under which reason code, and on what date?
3. What ratio and calculation did you use, and which months and merchant ID?
4. What remediation or evidence do you need from me, and by what deadline?

If you send me last month's chargebacks and transactions, I can work out your Mastercard ratio. The two lines to compare it with are 150 bps and 100 chargebacks for ECM, and 300 bps and 300 chargebacks for HECM. That ratio won't tell you whether you're on MATCH, though, because MATCH uses a 3-month total and a USD amount. If a listing is real, a lawyer can advise you beyond what your provider tells you.
