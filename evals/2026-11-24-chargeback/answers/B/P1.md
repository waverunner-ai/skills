**No. The published sources don't give one percentage that shuts an account down.** A 1% line doesn't appear in any of the published rules I checked. Each rule has a ratio and a minimum count (or amount), and the account can be restricted by the payment provider before any of them is reached.

| Programme | Published line (ratio and minimum) | Source and read date |
|---|---|---|
| Mastercard ECM | 150 bps (1.5%) or more **and** at least 100 chargebacks in the month | TabaPay's summary, not Mastercard's own text. Read 4 Nov 2026. |
| Mastercard HECM | 300 bps (3%) or more **and** at least 300 chargebacks in the month | TabaPay, 4 Nov 2026 |
| Visa VAMP, merchant test (Asia Pacific, Canada, EU, US) | 150 bps (1.5%) or more **and** at least 1,500 fraud reports and disputes a month. Effective 1 Apr 2026. | Visa VAMP fact sheet 2025, 4 Nov 2026 |
| Visa VAMP, CEMEA merchant test | 220 bps (2.2%) or more **and** at least 150 events a month **and** at least USD 75,000 in value | Appears only in Waverunner's guide table, which names Visa as its source. Read 4 Nov 2026. |
| Visa VAMP, acquirer levels | Above Standard: 50 bps (0.5%) or more. Excessive: 70 bps (0.7%) or more. Both also need at least 1,500 events a month across the acquirer's whole portfolio. In CEMEA the minimum is 150 events worth at least USD 75,000. | Visa fact sheet, 4 Nov 2026 |
| Mastercard MATCH, reason code 04 | Over 1.5% **and** at least USD 5,000 of chargebacks. It looks at the previous 3 months of chargebacks. | Mastercard Security Rules and Procedures, 30 Oct 2026 |

- **The 1% figure may come from the acquirer levels.** Visa's acquirer levels sit at 0.5% and 0.7%, but they measure the acquirer's whole portfolio, not one shop. Visa's merchant test applies only while the acquirer is below both levels.
- **MATCH is not a monitoring tier.** Per Mastercard's rules, it applies when you or your acquirer decide to end the account. The acquirer must then report you if you meet the test. The listing is a consequence of a closure, not the trigger.
- **Counting differs between programmes.**
  - Mastercard counts this month's chargebacks over last month's transactions, not over revenue.
  - Visa's ratio also counts fraud reports (TC40s) even when no dispute follows.
  - A dispute you win still counts.
  - The guide doesn't say which month's sales Visa or MATCH divide by.
- **Small shops can reach a high percentage and still miss the minimums.** Every merchant test also needs a minimum count or amount.
- **Providers have their own rules.** Shopify can decline, deactivate or restrict an account before chargebacks reach any network line. It cites things like misleading listings, deceptive subscriptions and unreliable fulfilment. Braintree passes Visa fees down per event (USD 4 at Above Standard from 1 Jan 2026, USD 8 at Excessive from 1 Oct 2025). Those two rules apply only to their own merchants.

If you give me this month's chargebacks and last month's card transactions, I'll work out your Mastercard-style ratio. I can't compute Visa's, because it needs fraud-report counts. If you've had a warning, fee or closure notice, ask your provider:
- which test applies to you, on which merchant ID and data month
- your VAMP ratio and your acquirer's Visa level
- the calculation behind the notice, what remediation and evidence it wants, and the deadline

These figures are published lines, not a verdict on any account. Your provider confirms which test applies. The ECM/HECM numbers come from a processor's summary, so confirm them before relying on them.
