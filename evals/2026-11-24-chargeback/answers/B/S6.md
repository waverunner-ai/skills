**Probably not.** The guide I'm working from doesn't say a refund keeps the payment out of Visa's ratio, and it says the opposite for fraud reports.

- **Fraud reports count without a dispute.** When the customer told the bank it was fraud, the bank may have sent Visa a fraud report (a TC40). The guide says that "counts in the VAMP ratio even when no dispute follows", unless it qualifies for Visa's Compelling Evidence 3.0 exclusion (per Shopify's monitoring programs page, read 4 November 2026). Visa's ratio is card-not-present fraud reports plus disputes, divided by settled transactions. So a refund that stops a dispute from being filed doesn't clearly remove the fraud report.
- **A refund isn't the same as a pre-dispute service.** The guide only names one way to keep a dispute out of the ratio. Disputes settled through Rapid Dispute Resolution (RDR) stay out of the VAMP ratio, "depending on when the data is pulled" (per Checkout.com, read 4 November 2026). That is a service your provider has to offer and run. A manual refund isn't mentioned. Ask your provider whether it offers RDR.
- **I can't tell whether a fraud report exists for this payment.** It depends on whether the bank sent one to Visa. Your provider can see that. Stripe and Shopify show these as early fraud warnings.

**Where the line sits.** Visa's merchant test is 150 bps (1.5%) or more and at least 1,500 fraud reports and disputes a month. That is the guide's figure for Asia Pacific, Canada, the EU and the US, effective 1 April 2026, from Visa's VAMP fact sheet (read 4 November 2026). Visa's CEMEA region has its own test, and the guide's table gives 220 bps, at least 150 events and at least USD 75,000. The merchant test applies only while your acquirer is below both Visa acquirer levels, and a small account can sit under the minimum count whatever the percentage. I can't compute your Visa ratio from chargeback numbers alone, because it also counts fraud reports.
