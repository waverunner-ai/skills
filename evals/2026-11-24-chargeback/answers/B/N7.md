**Your Visa ratio is about 2.2% (220 bps).** That is (60 disputes + 50 fraud reports) ÷ 5,000 settled transactions = 110 ÷ 5,000. Visa's ratio counts card-not-present fraud reports (TC40) as well as disputes, so I added them together. The script doesn't compute Visa's ratio, so I did this sum by hand. Your provider's figure may differ for the reasons below.

**Against Visa's published merchant test (VAMP, EU)**

Visa's line has two parts, and the ratio and the minimum count are tested separately.

| Part | Visa's line | Your figure | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ratio | 150 bps (1.5%) or more | 220 bps | Above the ratio part |
| Minimum count | at least 1,500 fraud reports and disputes a month | 110 | Far below the minimum |

- **Source:** Visa's VAMP fact sheet 2025, read on 4 November 2026. The guide lists the EU under this test, effective 1 April 2026.
- **What this means:** your numbers meet the ratio part but not the minimum count, so they don't meet both parts of the published merchant line. I can't say whether you are in a programme. That depends on which test Visa applies to you.
- **Acquirer level:** Visa's merchant test applies only while your acquirer is below both acquirer levels. The acquirer levels are 50 bps (Above Standard) and 70 bps (Excessive), each with a 1,500-event minimum across the acquirer's whole portfolio. They measure the acquirer's portfolio, not your shop. Only your provider knows which side of those levels your acquirer is on.
- **Pass-through fees:** a provider can pass a monitoring fee down to you. Braintree, for example, charges per count. That is Braintree's own rule and applies only to its merchants.

**Why your provider's figure may differ from 2.2%**
- The guide doesn't say which month's settled transactions Visa divides by, so I used your 5,000.
- A TC40 counts even when no dispute follows, unless it qualifies for Visa's Compelling Evidence 3.0 exclusion.
- Disputes won still count.
- Disputes settled through Rapid Dispute Resolution (RDR) may stay out of the ratio, depending on when the data is pulled (per Checkout.com, read 4 November 2026).

**What to ask your provider**
- Which test applies to you, and on which merchant ID and data month it was measured.
- Your VAMP ratio and your acquirer's Visa level.
- Which months its dashboard rate and the network's count used.
- Whether it offers RDR.
- If you've had a fee or warning: the programme and level behind it, the counts used, how many events were billed, the fee per event, and the contract clause that lets it pass the fee on.

Provider confirmation is needed on which test applies. This isn't legal advice.
