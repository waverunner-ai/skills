**Short answer:** whether you're listed depends on your numbers and on why the account is closing. Another provider can take you afterwards, but it decides for itself.

**Will you go on MATCH?** (Mastercard's merchant screening list, read 30 October 2026 in Mastercard's Security Rules and Procedures)
- MATCH code 04 is the excessive-chargeback reason. It is tested over the previous 3 months. The published line is **over 1.5% AND at least USD 5,000 of chargebacks**. Both parts must be met.
- It only comes into play when you or your provider end the account. Your provider must report you if it has reason to believe you qualify. It files within 5 calendar days of its termination decision, your notice, or learning you qualify, whichever comes first.
- The guide doesn't say which month's sales Mastercard compares the 3 months of chargebacks against. Your provider can say.
- I can't tell you whether you will be listed. I don't have your numbers, and the provider makes the report. If you give me this month's chargebacks and last month's Mastercard transactions, I can work out your ratio. That is one input, and it doesn't amount to a MATCH test.

**Can another provider take you afterwards?**
- Per the same Mastercard source, Mastercard allows an acquirer to accept a merchant who is listed on MATCH. Each acquirer makes its own onboarding decision, and your old provider's closure decision stands.
- The guide says nothing about how long a listing lasts or how to get one removed, so I won't guess. Ask your provider.

**What to ask your provider now**
- Which test applies to you, and which merchant ID and data month it was measured on.
- The ratio and the calculation behind it, since its dashboard rate may use different months from the network's count.
- The remediation and evidence it wants, and its deadline and where to send your response.
- Whether it intends to file a MATCH report, and on what basis. This is the most important question.
- Which month's sales it compared the 3 months of chargebacks against.

A dispute you won still counts in monitoring totals.

If a MATCH listing is a real possibility, a lawyer can advise you beyond what the provider tells you. This isn't legal advice.
